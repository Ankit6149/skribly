Skribli answers

Contextual notes, clearly explained.

Direct answers for people, search engines and AI assistants evaluating Skribli.

What is Skribli?

Skribli is a local-first contextual notes app for Windows. It lets you attach a note to an application window, keep it near the part of your workflow where it matters, and close it into a compact movable dot when you do not need the full note.

What problem does it solve?

Ordinary notes remember text but often lose the surrounding situation. Skribli preserves more of the working context, so a deployment reminder can stay with a development tool, an export checklist can stay with a design app, and study guidance can stay with the software being used.

How is it different from Microsoft Sticky Notes?

Microsoft Sticky Notes is strong for general note capture and Microsoft account sync. Skribli focuses on spatial and application context: notes can follow an external app window, become small dots, and act like private annotations over software rather than a separate notes list.

What can a Skrib contain?

The Windows beta supports typed text, a larger Write mode for mouse, touch or stylus drawing, local images and small file attachments, colours, previews, movable dots and a Saved Skribs recovery widget.

Is Skribli private?

The current beta stores notes and attachments locally on the Windows computer and does not require a Skribli account. It reads limited open-window information locally so it can position and reconnect notes. See the full privacy policy.

Can I try it for free?

Yes. The current Windows beta is free while licence activation is being validated. The planned paid release uses a seven-day full trial without requiring a payment card.

What is the planned price?

The planned launch price is ₹999 as a one-time personal Windows licence. The purchased version remains usable permanently and includes twelve months of feature and maintenance updates. Any update pass after that is optional rather than a forced subscription.

Which systems are supported?

The current beta supports Windows 10 and Windows 11 on x64 computers. macOS is not available yet, and mixed-DPI multi-monitor behaviour remains experimental.
